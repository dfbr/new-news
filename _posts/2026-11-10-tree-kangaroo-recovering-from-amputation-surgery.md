title: Tree kangaroo recovering from amputation surgery
---

Posted by Gigi Allianic, CommunicationsPoppy, a Matschie's tree kangaroo at Woodland Park Zoo, is recovering from amputation surgery performed last week in response to a bacterial infection. After observing the rapidly-spreading bacteria during a CT scan, a team of animal health experts removed Poppy’s right arm.Poppy in October 2026, prior to surgery. Photo by Lindsay Phillips/Woodland Park Zoo.The female marsupial will turn 3 next month and arrived at the zoo in June under a recommendation by the Tree Kangaroo Species Survival Plan. It is unknown when or how Poppy originally contracted the bacteria, but it is not contagious and no other animals or humans are at risk of contracting it from her. She lives in a habitat that is not viewable to zoo guests and is recovering in an off-view space where her animal care team can observe her closely and shower her with lots of extra love.Tree kangaroos are arboreal marsupials that spend nearly their entire lives in trees and are equipped with exceptional climbing and jumping skills. In late August, Poppy's animal care team observed the tree kangaroo holding up her right front foot while navigating her habitat. The zoo's animal health team performed a diagnostic examination under anesthesia and determined a bacterial infection was present in one of the bones of her forearm. Since her original diagnosis, Poppy has been under extensive treatment with multiple antibiotics and pain medications and has been continuously monitored by her animal care and animal health teams. Despite this treatment the tree kangaroo showed little to no improvement in the mobility of her arm. It was determined that additional diagnostics and possible surgery to clean away the bone were needed for continued treatment. The zoo worked with skilled and compassionate external veterinary teams including Dr. Duane Robinson, a veterinary surgeon with Boreal Animal Surgery located in Renton, Wash., and Dr. Chris Bailey and the team at Timberline Veterinary Emergency &amp; Specialty in Seattle to perform a CT scan for further diagnostics.Poppy in June 2026. Photo by Fritz Comden/Woodland Park Zoo."Based on ongoing observation of her wellbeing and the spread of the infection revealed in the CT scan, Poppy’s health care team and animal keepers determined that amputation was the best option in a difficult situation. We’re hoping for the best because we recognize that this is a precarious situation for Poppy,” said Dr. Laura St. Clair, Associate Veterinarian at Woodland Park Zoo. “During this morning’s check-up Poppy showed positive signs of healing, and we are cautiously optimistic that she can adjust to a new life with three limbs."“Thanks to the daily care provided by our expert, dedicated animal care team, their sharp observational skills alerted us to the challenges Poppy was experiencing. They will give her extra attention in the coming days and months as she heals and adapts,” said Erin Sullivan, Animal Curator at Woodland Park Zoo. “Poppy is adjusting to her new life fairly well, considering the circumstances. Animals are very resilient and can surprise us in many ways. She is showing us how strong she is and how well she can adapt,” said Amanda Dukart, Animal Keeper at Woodland Park Zoo. “Watching her recovery, I am personally amazed and inspired by her. While we are still keeping a very close eye on her during her recovery, we have hope that she can have a good life after this time of healing. We are taking things day by day at this point.”Guests looking at a tree kangaroo in Forest Trailhead. Photo by Jeremy Dwyer-Lindgren/Woodland Park Zoo.Visitors may visit and discover Matschie's tree kangaroos in the all-new Forest Trailhead, which opened in May. The new exhibit is also home to red panda, kea—alpine parrots native to New Zealand—and a variety of reptiles, amphibians and fish species. Species Survival Plans are cooperative programs across accredited zoos and aquariums to help ensure healthy, self-sustaining populations of threatened and endangered species.
